By accessing this site or using the VitalTrek platform, an adventure tour operator and the people it authorizes agree to the conditions described here. If an organization does not accept these conditions, it should not use the platform.

Because VitalTrek is an academic MVP, these Terms may change as the product evolves through each sprint. The version published on this page is always the current one.

What VitalTrek does

VitalTrek helps adventure tour operators follow the progress of expedition groups along routes where mobile coverage is partial or absent. Wearable devices carried by participants record position and basic activity data, and that data is synchronized when a group reaches a Bluetooth checkpoint.

The platform then shows the operator an updated picture of each group: which checkpoints were reached, at what time, and which groups are behind their expected schedule.

Safety limitations

VitalTrek is not an emergency service, a rescue service, or a medical device. It must never be used as the only safety measure for an expedition, and it does not replace satellite communicators, emergency beacons, trained guides, or established rescue protocols.

Information reaches the platform only when a group passes a checkpoint. Between two checkpoints there is no live tracking, and a delay in the data does not necessarily mean an incident has occurred — nor does the absence of an alert mean a group is safe.

Terrain, weather, battery life, device damage and radio interference can all prevent a synchronization from happening. Operators must plan for those situations and keep independent emergency procedures in place.

Operator responsibilities

The tour operator remains fully responsible for the safety of its expeditions. When using VitalTrek, an operator agrees to:

  • Inform every participant that wearable devices will record location and activity data, and obtain their consent before the expedition starts.
  • Keep emergency plans, communication equipment and qualified guides independent of the platform.
  • Provide accurate route, checkpoint and group information, and keep it updated.
  • Restrict access to the platform to authorized staff and protect their credentials.
  • Use the data only to coordinate the safety and logistics of the expedition.

Participant responsibilities

Participants are asked to carry the assigned wearable device during the whole route, to follow the instructions of their guide, and to report any damage or malfunction of the device as soon as they notice it.

Participation in the monitoring is voluntary. A participant may ask the operator to stop collecting their data, and the operator must explain how that affects the expedition's safety procedures.

Accounts and devices

Accounts are issued to operator organizations, not to individuals, and each organization decides which of its members may access them. Sharing credentials outside the authorized team is not permitted.

Wearables and checkpoint units provided as part of the service remain the property of VitalTrek unless a separate agreement states otherwise, and must be returned in working condition.

Professional and ethical commitment

The team develops VitalTrek following the ACM/IEEE-CS Software Engineering Code of Ethics and Professional Practice and the Code of Ethics of the Colegio de Ingenieros del Perú. In practice, this means we accept the following commitments:

  • Public interest first: we state the safety limits of the product plainly rather than overstating what it can do.
  • Honesty about the product: features that are planned are described as planned, and this site does not claim results the MVP has not achieved.
  • Respect for the people being monitored: data collection is explained, consented to, and limited to what an expedition's safety requires.
  • Accountability of the team: every change is traceable in our public GitHub repositories through reviewed pull requests.
  • Inclusion: the product is built to be usable in English and Latin American Spanish and to meet accessibility guidelines.
